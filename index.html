The Institute for
Responsible Superintelligence

RESI is an independent nonprofit research institute based in Cambridge, Massachusetts, building the scientific foundations for making superintelligence safe by design.

Safe by design means safety properties are specified in advance and achieved by mechanisms whose guarantees can be analyzed before deployment.

As AI systems become more capable, we need to understand what will keep them trustworthy and under human control. RESI brings technical, legal, and economic research together around that question, drawing on the scientific approach that transformed modern cryptography.

The modern cryptography precedent

Long ago, encryption schemes were ad hoc: constantly broken, red-teamed, and patched. Modern cryptography began when researchers formulated definitions of computational security and invented primitives that could provably satisfy them under explicit mathematical assumptions: public-key encryption, digital signatures, zero-knowledge proofs, multi-party computation, and obfuscation. These inventions have withstood decades of attacks and enormous increases in computing power.

RESI is a bet that such guarantees are possible for superintelligence safety, and that it will happen by bringing together researchers who aim their efforts at responsible superintelligence, using frontier AI tools.

Key research areas

  • Verification and oversight

    What can a less capable overseer reliably verify about a more capable AI’s properties, answers, and actions without reproducing all of the system’s work?

  • Control and delegation

    What authority can we delegate to AI while preserving our ability to intervene as AI becomes more intelligent than us?

  • Composition

    Which safety properties can be better achieved when connections between models, tools, and agents are limited?

  • Shaping multi-agent dynamics

    How should we design agents and structure their interactions to build safe multi-agent systems with provable guarantees?
