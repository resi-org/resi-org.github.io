## Observed patterns

**There is no single peer format.** [Resolution](https://resolution.org/), [METR](https://metr.org/blog/), [Transluce](https://transluce.org/news), and [Redwood](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/blog) give compact explanatory copy. [CHAI's homepage](https://humancompatible.ai/), [LawAI's blog](https://law-ai.org/blog/), and [FMF's publication index](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/publications/) rely much more on titles and metadata. Description-bearing samples range from very short identifiers to substantial abstracts. [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research) offers long research abstracts; that serves a research catalogue rather than a lightweight news list.

**Useful short descriptions explain a contribution or question.** The sampled [METR](https://metr.org/blog/) listings tell readers what was investigated or reviewed. [Transluce](https://transluce.org/news) gives short findings or purposes. [Apart's research index](https://apartresearch.com/research) identifies what a benchmark measures or what a method detects. None requires a sentence asserting that every development is important or exciting. RESI can explain relevance through the subject and connection to a researcher.

**Names serve different roles.** [ARC](https://www.alignment.org/) and [Redwood](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/blog) use author lines. [CSET's coverage listings](https://cset.georgetown.edu/news/) identify whose expertise or authorship connects an external article to the institution. [Apollo](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog) names its CEO in a testimony description while technical listings can foreground the topic. [IAS](https://www.ias.edu/news) gives appointment/relationship information when reporting on scholars. [CHAI's News archive](https://humancompatible.ai/news/) also identifies authors and affiliate roles, while its Home listings omit descriptions. A writer's byline, a quoted expert, a co-author and a research lead are different facts; the comparison gives no basis for routinely calling all named researchers project leaders.

**Homepage selection often includes editorial features.** [GovAI](https://www.governance.ai/) features a mixed selection while its [Updates](https://www.governance.ai/updates) archive has different contents. [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/) has featured work and a separate latest list. [Simons](https://simons.berkeley.edu/homepage) and [IHES](https://www.ihes.fr/en/) show event information before news in distinct sections. These observations do not establish an automatic event-priority rule elsewhere. RESI's explicit upcoming-event priority and three-entry capacity govern RESI regardless of these examples.

**News and events can be connected without being identical.** FAR.AI mixes event reports with research in [News](https://www.far.ai/blog) but has a separate event directory; GovAI includes event summaries in Updates. Such retrospective reporting is distinct from an upcoming attendance invitation. RESI's existing event classification and Rachel's retrospective opt-in must remain intact.

**Attribution and qualification are the strongest defensible practices.** Preserve the source, researcher role, real date, status of the work and limits needed to avoid changing its meaning. These are sound scientific/editorial practices, especially relevant to AI safety claims. This sample does not establish a universal AI-safety-specific description length, naming rule, image requirement or ranking convention.

## Recommended RESI conventions

1. **Retain title, truthful date, source and linked original.** Preserve the original headline on News. Use a shorter homepage title only where it improves scanning. Keep established stable News anchors.
2. **Use 30–50 words as a flexible News default.** Usually two sentences: identify the development and RESI connection, then explain the specific contribution or scope. A straightforward item can be shorter. A technical caveat can justify modestly more space. Avoid a fixed minimum or filler significance sentence.
3. **Use 12–25 words on Home.** One factual sentence with validated fallback to the News description when no separate text is supplied. The same record drives both contexts. No new image or card treatment is needed for this item.
4. **Name people when the connection matters.** Miranda should be named here because her co-authorship and affiliation explain why RESI is listing the report. Other co-authors remain credited at the source; list them locally when their participation materially changes the story. Do not imply Miranda led the work or that RESI commissioned/funded it.
5. **Separate quotation from paraphrase.** Quote only exact sourced language. A source label must not cause a RESI-written summary to be displayed as a quotation. The Globe's existing quoted excerpt must remain quoted.
6. **Classify this as external research involving a RESI researcher.** It is not press coverage or an institute-owned research claim. The current two-category model needs a small extension or clearer replacement; visible metadata can continue to read News · Anthropic · date without redesign.
7. **Keep one development together.** If later coverage reports on the same FlashProve release, prefer adding a clearly attributed related link to the existing entry over multiple nearly identical announcements. A materially separate development can warrant a new dated record. This is a recommendation, not a documented universal peer convention.
8. **Keep routine updates lightweight.** Verify the primary source, researcher role, date and limits; check duplicates; prepare one recommended entry; verify Home's actual selected three and News/Search; record only material decisions. Research the reference collection again when a new editorial question requires it.

## Configuration assessment before the first addition

Current local implementation inspected: content/updates/, src/content.config.ts, src/lib/announcements.ts, src/lib/event-announcement-policy.ts, HomePage.astro, news.astro and search.astro. The site currently loads one set of records, sorts all News/events together by announcement date, filters homepage eligibility and slices three. News is unconditionally eligible; only Events supports explicit exclusion. Equal dates have no explicit editorial tie-breaker. The updates README still describes older selection/link behavior.

The inspected live Home retrieval showed workshop → September 18 LessWrong → Boston Globe. That matches the existing dates but does **not** prove event priority. The proposed report and workshop both have November 2 publication/announcement dates. In the current loader, updates are concatenated before events; a stable date-only sort would therefore put this new News item **before** the workshop. A newer news date would also outrank it. No fixture was added to the application to demonstrate this; it follows directly from the inspected code.

Recommended scoped implementation, already authorized as a requirement in E10 but outside this research-only phase:

- Give News an explicit default inclusion policy and exclusion option. Represent a genuinely unresolved promotion as held/excluded with a recorded reason for Rachel; missing optional Home copy is not a hold. Keep held News records on News if otherwise publishable.
- Centralize Home selection: eligible upcoming events first; then eligible News and explicitly opted-in past-event retrospectives by truthful announcement date, with a stable ID tie-breaker; slice at three only after filtering/sorting. Newest announcement first within the upcoming-event tier is a proposed deterministic default. The precise position of an approved retrospective relative to News is not specified by Rachel's current rules; ordinary date order below upcoming events is the recommendation to resolve when such an item is first needed.
- Preserve event routing, explicit exclusions, Cambridge date boundaries, retrospective opt-in, archive records, stable anchors and approved treatments.
- Separate publisher/source attribution and summary quotation mode; introduce an accurate research classification. Validate required source/date/title/summary for ordinary additions without breaking approved legacy records. Preserve external headline links and existing Home links to News for non-press items unless a deliberate change is approved.
- Update updates/event contribution READMEs, AGENTS and shared records to match the implemented policy. Reuse test:announcements with meaningful cases for automatic inclusion, fallback, upcoming priority, three-slot capacity, explicit News exclusion/hold, equal dates, historical dates, event expiry, retrospective opt-in and displaced News remaining in News/Search. Run check/build and inspect actual Home/News/Search plus desktop/mobile layouts and metadata before claiming implementation verified.
