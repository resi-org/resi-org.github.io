# Authorship wording in RESI reference organizations

Historical research snapshot: subsequent item-copy approval and implementation status are tracked in [WORKLIST NEWS1/E10](../../WORKLIST.md). The observations and draft below are retained as evidence of the research stage.

Follow-up requested November 4, 2026. Rachel asked for reference evidence about introducing Miranda Christ's co-authorship, especially the difference between direct authorship language and saying someone was named as a co-author. This is a wording comparison, not approval of the existing draft or an application change.

## What the reference evidence supports

The examples establish several ordinary constructions: a researcher co-authored a paper; a researcher is co-author of a paper; a researcher was an author on a paper; a new study was co-authored by named researchers; and named researchers show or explore a result. They do not establish a uniquely best sentence, nor that one construction is empirically more compelling to readers.

No comparable reference news example using the proposed named-as construction was found. That is a bounded search finding, not proof that no reference ever uses it. The distinction in meaning is that direct verbs state authorship/contribution, whereas named-as language foregrounds the recording of a credit. This interpretation of the language should not be presented as a measured audience effect or a claim that the person's contribution is smaller.

Targeted authorship searches covered 23 registry domains: CSET, Santa Fe, Perimeter, IAS, LawAI, GovAI, Simons, IHES, Transluce, FAR.AI, Apollo, Stanford HAI, Epoch, Rethink Priorities, Cooperative AI Foundation, ARC, Resolution, Lightcone, METR, Redwood, IAPS, CAIS and Astera. Epoch and Rethink have examples in broader reports/impact copy; these are secondary context. One Perimeter article returned 403; another relevant Perimeter article was readable and is cited above. The initial broad exact-phrase search found authorship-policy material outside the registry; that material does not establish the reference organizations' News practice and is excluded from the comparison.

Re-read Resolution, Lightcone and ARC organizational pages alongside the initial 44-organization comparison. Resolution's compact homepage descriptions generally foreground the development, with authors on destinations. Lightcone has no comparable chronological organizational paper-announcement list. ARC uses explicit bylines and research excerpts. Those structures cannot resolve the exact Miranda sentence by themselves.

## Application to the pending Miranda draft

The reference evidence supports both **co-authored a report** and **is a co-author of a report**. The proposed named-as wording has no comparable news precedent in this sample. Direct co-authorship does not imply leading the study. Naming the publisher separately follows the closest CSET example, and avoids relying on the vague object work when the item is specifically a report.

Institution-first and name-first syntax both occur: CSET uses the institution's possessive with the researcher; IAS uses the name followed by the institutional appointment; Santa Fe places the institutional role immediately before the name. A simple role-before-name construction does not need commas around the name. A name-first construction can set off the role in commas. This is a grammatical distinction, not evidence that one performs better.

These findings refine the basis for review; they do not replace the pending draft, approve Miranda's preferred wording or authorize publication. No message was sent to Miranda.
