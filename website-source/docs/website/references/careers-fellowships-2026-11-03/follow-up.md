## What earns space in Work at RESI

The recommendations below are editorial judgments informed by the examples; they do not transfer another organization's policies to RESI.

**Resolution: make hiring status actionable.** Its [Careers page](https://resolution.org/careers) distinguishes active, paused and closed areas and links to detailed roles. The [Research Scientist listing](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/resolution/65704205-7ccd-4df6-a7d1-3e3338bd80a8) gives duties, qualifications, terms and process. A dated status helps applicants understand what submitting now means. The institute's October 28 update and the listing's August 21 text do not fully agree, so RESI should maintain one authoritative current statement rather than duplicate dated updates.

**Lightcone: describe the actual work and fit.** Its [jobs gateway](https://www.lightconeinfrastructure.com/jobs.html) is little more than a current-hiring statement and three role links. The [generalist role](https://www.lightconeinfrastructure.com/core-team-generalist.html) explains the variety of work, frequent team changes, practical skills and explicit cultural preferences. The [campus operations role](https://www.lightconeinfrastructure.com/campus-operations-co-lead.html) makes the work concrete through tasks. The useful lesson is specificity: readers can assess what the job entails and whether they would enjoy it. Its unusually intense cultural expectations are its own, not a template for RESI.

Broader checks support the same division: [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/careers) makes research culture concrete through work/discussion practices; [METR](https://metr.org/careers) connects roles to its research purpose; [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/about/careers) distinguishes roles from general interest and explains review expectations. ARC's role also specifies the background it values rather than relying on a generic mission invitation.

For RESI, the short card should carry:

1. The existing culture paragraph, if Rachel decides to move it: it belongs most directly with employment and contains a substantive commitment to junior researchers' credit/careers. Preserve `#culture` if moved.
2. One accurate, dated status sentence explaining whether applications are accepted/reviewed and for which role.
3. One link to complete role/application information once that destination exists. A full external listing can serve this purpose; a separate Jobs page is not required just to have a card.

Retain the existing two culture sentences for the initial proposal, rather than introducing new claims about eligibility, research freedom, compensation or mentoring:

> RESI’s culture is mission-first: open collaboration and early sharing of ideas. To protect junior researchers, we will actively support their credit and careers.

If confirmed, the useful status would state ongoing acceptance and review timing; the anticipated year-long search need not occupy the small card. Do not encourage candidates to wait a year to apply.

## AcademicJobsOnline: full platform versus eDelivery

Adam's forwarded example identifies AcademicJobsOnline and demonstrates an applicant-initiated request to a referee, with writer-submitted reusable or tailored letters. It does not identify a selected RESI plan or establish that an employer account exists. Private writer links/access details are not public application destinations and were not used.

| Option | Job listing | Applications and references | Employer review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Full service](https://academicjobsonline.org/ajo/intro) | Hosted position ad | Configurable application forms/documents/questions; applicants invite referees to submit letters, with writers controlling their letters | Online access for committee members, shortlists, comments/ratings, exports/downloads |
| [Free eDelivery](https://academicjobsonline.org/ajo/request/4) | No hosted ad | Employer-specific link receives application portfolios, including reference letters; notifications and PDF/ZIP delivery | No online application-management/review workspace |

Thus RESI could put a job description on its own site and link to a full AJO listing/application, or retain the listing and review process elsewhere and link to eDelivery for dossiers. eDelivery is more than a standalone reference-letter upload widget, but it is not the complete recruitment platform. Applicants should not be asked to attach their own confidential letters. The writer submits them through their own invitation/access.

For a single shared listing/application/reference/review workflow, full service is the closer fit to the question Rachel asked. For convenient dossier delivery with review handled outside AJO, eDelivery is the lighter option. The [membership page](https://academicjobsonline.org/ajo/membertypes) distinguishes account types and costs; the small-research-group option has postdoc/student restrictions and should not be assumed to cover an unrestricted researcher role. Choosing a plan, activating an account and deciding when/how many letters to request are separate operational decisions. No account was created or application/reference request submitted.
