What the expanded review changes

It qualifies the earlier answer. Most AI-safety references did not expose a general visitor roster on the public pages reviewed. However, explicitly naming visiting scholars or fellows is an established option within AI-focused organizations, not just a practice of traditional institutes.

Five of the 37 core references clearly expose a visitor section or visitor-labelled team entries: Forethought, GovAI, LawAI, CSER and LCFI. This is a count of confirmed public presentation, not a claim that the other organizations never host or name visitors.

The stronger observed pattern concerns the relationship being documented. Visitor-centered institutes maintain structured records; other organizations use named appointments, cohorts, mentors, speakers, collaborators or testimonials. ARC and Constellation explicitly host visitors, without a comprehensive roster being found. There is insufficient evidence here for a simple newer-versus-older rule.

Prominent names usually have a defined context. SAIF/IDAIS labels named dialogue signatories; CAIS distinguishes fellows from guest speakers; Simons attaches visitors to institutions and programs. No reviewed example establishes a published rule that fame alone warrants inclusion after an informal visit.

Findings to share with Adam

Across the full AI-safety reference set, most organizations do not publish a general visitor list, but several—including Forethought, GovAI and LawAI—explicitly name visiting scholars or fellows. Institutes such as Simons, IAS and IHES maintain more extensive visitor directories because visiting research is central to their model; elsewhere, networks are often shown through fellowship cohorts, collaborators, speakers and testimonials. The clearest common thread is a defined relationship or activity attached to each name, rather than a clear divide between newer and older organizations.

Scope and method

The starting collection was reconciled against the saved website surveys, deep dives and later organization registry. Review covered public home/navigation, people/about, and relevant program, visitor, fellowship, event and archival pages. Targeted web searches were used to investigate unclear cases and distinguish historical visits from current directories. This is an organization-by-organization public-presentation audit, not an exhaustive crawl of every page or a survey of every AI-safety organization in existence.

CHAI, CSER, LCFI and CSET remain because of their direct AI / existential-risk relevance. General university centers do not drive the conclusion. Simons is retained because its theoretical-computer-science visitor model is particularly relevant to RESI. IAS, IHES and Perimeter add formal research-visitor models; Santa Fe adds interdisciplinary appointments; Astera and THE NEW INSTITUTE add newer independent residency examples. THE NEW INSTITUTE is explicitly treated as historical.

Interpretation limits: “No directory found” means none was located in the reviewed public presentation; it does not mean there are no visitors. Search and page extraction can miss unindexed or dynamically loaded content. A fellowship, remote affiliation, named signatory or seminar appearance is not automatically a physical visit. Website labels and dates were recorded, not independently verified with the institutions. No website changes were made.
