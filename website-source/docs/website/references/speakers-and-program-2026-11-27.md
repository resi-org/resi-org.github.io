# Speaker portraits and programs: comparative research

Researched November 27, 2026 for Rachel's question about Adam's proposal to integrate portraits into the December 4 schedule. Research only: no application edits, preview redesign, content approvals or publication. This supplements the November 13 event-page research, whose observations were historical leads rather than fresh verification.

## Conclusion

There is no single mandatory convention. A recurring research-event pattern separates an identity roster (name, affiliation, sometimes portrait) from chronological sessions (time, title, presenter). Integrated portraits also have strong institutional precedent. Repeated names are ordinary. Full title repetition occurs, including at Simons Foundation, but is especially useful when the second view adds an abstract, slides, recording or biography. RESI currently repeats substantial presentation information without that extra depth.

For RESI, the strongest conservative recommendation is an early compact photo roster with names/affiliations, followed by a single detailed program containing talk titles. It preserves early recognition of the community while removing the long duplicated speaker/talk inventory. Adam's integrated alternative is also worth a preview: time above each existing portrait-and-copy composition, short utility rows for breaks, panel photo groups within their timed sessions. Integration does not require tiny thumbnails or a spreadsheet-like table. These are design judgments, not measured conversion findings or approved changes.

## Method and limitations

Reviewed 23 distinct event examples, including associated overview, speaker, schedule and archive views; these are not 23 independent design systems. Nine peer-event examples were additionally inspected in the rendered browser (Simons Institute Safety-Guaranteed LLMs; archived Big Data and Differential Privacy; Simons Foundation MPS; Stanford Hoffman-Yee and AI+Policy; RAND Health Workforce; FAR.AI London; Anthropic Partner Summit; OpenAI Forum Deep Research). RESI's actual live page was also freshly inspected: 12 main speaker portrait entries and a later 26-entry program; representative long talk titles appear twice.

Judgments about scanning, appeal and page length are qualitative. No visitor analytics, conversion results or controlled usability studies were available. Several examples are post-event archives, and some closed workshops do not publish a full public timetable. Absence of a public agenda is not proof of a choice about agenda design. Browser review resolved extracted hidden template states at Anthropic and omitted person cards at Stanford. Do not count those hidden states as visible duplication.

## Implications for the next preview (not approved implementation)

- Give the speaker overview and program distinct functions if both remain. A compact roster can keep all faces visible early while titles appear once in the program.
- Alternatively merge the existing portrait-and-text compositions into chronological sessions. Put time above the content at RESI's narrow column width; avoid adding a permanent third column that squeezes long titles. Retain appreciable portraits rather than reducing everyone to tiny avatars. Breaks/lunch can remain much shorter rows; photo height need not encode duration.
- Panels should have one timed session heading, with their smaller portrait group and moderator line inside it. Spotlights should remain a grouped block. Integration must not promote a panelist to a standalone speaker.
- If a photo-rich program grows tall, visible morning/afternoon navigation can help. Do not silently truncate titles or hide essential information merely to achieve equal-height cards.
- Keep stable talk/panel/spotlight/program anchors, credits, approved cropping and current affiliations; source one canonical set of session information wherever it is displayed.
- Repetition is justified by a distinct visitor task, not simply by the prestige of an organization using it. It is also a maintenance risk: MPS's agenda still says TBA for a session whose later abstract has a title.
